Medicare in Nicholasville and Jessamine County

Jessamine County has no full hospital of its own. That one fact should shape how you pick a Medicare plan here, and almost nobody explains why.

Austin Tyler wrote and reviewed this page. Licensed in Kentucky, NPN 20234188, Kentucky DOI licence #1187780. Plan counts come from the CMS files; the Saint Joseph network list came from the system itself. Both checked 18 August 2026.

Jessamine County has no full inpatient hospital

Saint Joseph Jessamine on Keene Road runs the county's only full-service 24-hour emergency room, and it is not a full hospital. It is a department of Saint Joseph Hospital in Lexington, with an emergency room, imaging, lab, physical rehabilitation and physician offices.

If you are admitted, you are going to Lexington. That is not a criticism of a very good facility. It is the single most important thing to understand before you choose a Medicare Advantage plan in this county.

Nicholasville is 11 to 13 miles from Lexington, about 16 to 22 minutes up US-27 depending on how Nicholasville Road is behaving. Close enough that most people here already use Lexington doctors without thinking twice about it.

But that changes what a Medicare Advantage network means for you. In a county with its own hospital, the Lexington networks matter for referrals. In Jessamine County, the Lexington networks are the whole question. Every overnight stay, every surgery, every specialist admission happens on somebody else's network.

How many Medicare Advantage plans does Jessamine County have?

Thirty-seven for 2026, of which 19 carry a $0 monthly premium. The average premium across all 37 is $13.55. Six carriers sell here: Humana, UnitedHealthcare, Anthem, WellCare, Aetna and Devoted Health.

There are another 25 Special Needs Plans, but most people cannot join those. They are limited to specific situations, usually having both Medicare and Medicaid.

Which Medicare Advantage plans does Saint Joseph accept?

All of them, as far as Jessamine County is concerned. CHI Saint Joseph Health accepts Medicare Advantage from Aetna, Anthem, Devoted Health (since 1 January 2025), Humana, Passport by Molina, UnitedHealthcare and WellCare.

Every one of the six carriers selling general-enrollment plans in Jessamine County is on that list. All 37 plans are accepted across the CHI Saint Joseph system, from the emergency room on Keene Road to Saint Joseph Hospital and Saint Joseph East in Lexington.

Verified with CHI Saint Joseph Health by Austin Tyler in August 2026. Networks change and individual plans within a carrier can differ, so we re-check before anyone enrolls. Passport by Molina appears on the accepted list but sells only a dual-eligible Special Needs Plan here, so it is relevant if you have both Medicare and Medicaid.

That is genuinely reassuring, and it is only half the picture. Because you will be admitted in Lexington, the hospital you end up at is not always the one you planned on. A specialist refers where a specialist refers.

The plans that work at Saint Joseph and fail at Baptist

Since January 2024, Baptist Health Lexington has not been in network with UnitedHealthcare or WellCare Medicare Advantage. The split followed a public disagreement over denied care and slow payment. Humana went back in network at Baptist in spring 2024; those other two did not.

Those two carriers sell 11 of the 37 plans available in Jessamine County. So roughly three in ten plans on your menu are fine throughout the Saint Joseph system and a problem at Baptist, which is a twenty-minute drive from Nicholasville and where a good number of Jessamine specialists admit.

If you worked for the railroad, your Medicare works differently

R.J. Corman Railroad Group is headquartered in Nicholasville, with around 1,600 employees across more than twenty states, so this county has more railroad families than most.

If you receive a railroad retirement annuity, your Medicare is handled by the Railroad Retirement Board, not the Social Security Administration. Enrollment in Parts A and B is generally automatic at 65 and the card arrives without an application. Your Part B premium comes out of the railroad annuity.

If you are not yet drawing that annuity when you turn 65, you contact an RRB field office to enroll. Not Social Security.

Source: U.S. Railroad Retirement Board, "Medicare for Railroad Workers and Their Families."

Two practical consequences. Your Medicare number and card come through a different agency, which trips people up when a plan asks for them. And when something goes wrong with Part B enrollment or premiums, the Social Security office is not the place to fix it. Railroad Medicare also uses a separate claims administrator on the Original Medicare side, which is worth knowing before you assume a billing problem is your plan's fault.

If you retired from the railroad and nobody has ever explained this to you, that is not unusual, and it is worth half an hour of somebody's time.

Retired from the schools, the county, or Asbury?

A lot of people retire in this county without ever shopping for Medicare, because Jessamine County Schools, the city and county payroll, Alltech and the two Asbury institutions in Wilmore all route retirees into group coverage. If that is you, start here rather than with the plan list above.

  • Retired teachers (TRS). The Medicare Eligible Health Plan moved its group Medicare Advantage coverage from UnitedHealthcare to Humana on 1 January 2026, with existing members enrolled automatically. Detail in our TRS MEHP guide.
  • State, county and classified school retirees (KPPA). The 2026 options are Medical Only, KPPA Essential and KPPA Premium, the latter two being Humana group Medicare Advantage PPOs that include drug coverage. Both require Part A and Part B. See our KPPA guide.

If either applies to you, most of the plan comparison above does not, and the real question is whether your retirement system coverage still fits.

Wilmore, and the veterans question

Wilmore is home to Thomson-Hood Veterans Center on Veterans Drive, Kentucky's first state veterans long-term care facility, with 285 beds providing skilled nursing, rehabilitation and memory care.

Veterans in this county ask us a version of the same question constantly: if I have VA benefits, do I still need Medicare? The short answer is that VA coverage and Medicare do not replace each other. VA benefits generally cover care at VA facilities, and Medicare covers care in the community. Having both is common and often sensible, and the interaction with Part B and with drug coverage is worth walking through properly rather than guessing at.

Where you fill prescriptions changed in 2025

Both Nicholasville Rite Aid stores closed when the chain liquidated nationwide during 2025, and prescription files moved elsewhere. If you filled at either, it is worth knowing exactly where your records went before you pick a drug plan.

This matters because every Part D and Medicare Advantage drug plan sorts pharmacies into preferred and standard, and the same prescription can cost noticeably less at a preferred counter. Nicholasville has Walgreens, CVS, two Kroger pharmacies and Walmart. Wilmore has Med-Save Pharmacy, locally owned and operating since 2004, which delivers.

No pharmacy in Nicholasville or Wilmore is open 24 hours. The nearest all-night options are in Lexington, which is worth knowing before a 2am prescription rather than during one.

So the honest advice here is not to chase the lowest premium. Tell us which counter you actually want to use, including if that is the independent in Wilmore, and we will find which plans treat it as preferred.

A Nicholasville address does not always mean Jessamine County

The Brannon Crossing area sits right on the Fayette line, uses Nicholasville 40356 addresses, and is about six miles from both downtown Lexington and downtown Nicholasville. People there shop and see doctors in Lexington and reasonably assume they are buying Lexington's Medicare plans.

They are not. Medicare Advantage and Part D plans are approved county by county, not by ZIP code or mailing address. If your property is in Jessamine County, you buy from Jessamine County's list of 37, whatever your address says and wherever you do your shopping.

This catches people every year, usually when a neighbor recommends a plan that turns out not to be sold on their side of the line. If you are anywhere near Brannon Road, it is worth confirming which county you are actually in before you compare anything.

Start with one question: where do your doctors admit?

In a county with no hospital of its own, that question does more work than any premium comparison. Your cardiologist admits somewhere. Your surgeon operates somewhere. Find out where, then find out whether the plan you are considering is welcome there.

Most people do not know the answer offhand, which is fine, because we can find out. Bring the names of your doctors and a list of your prescriptions with doses, and we will trace each one: who admits where, which of the 37 plans hold up at that hospital, what each prescription costs on each plan, and at which counter.
