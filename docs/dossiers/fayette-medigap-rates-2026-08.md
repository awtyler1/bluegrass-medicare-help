not warrant accuracy. So the site publishes **rounded ranges in our own words**, never the tool's

## 2. The five findings the article is built on

1. **Identical coverage, roughly double the price.** Mainstream Plan G for a 65-year-old man ran
   about $159 to $334. Plan G benefits are standardized in federal law, so every one of those is
   the same policy. The spread is the company, not the coverage.
2. **Plan G to Plan N is about $30 to $35 a month here**, roughly $400 a year, male or female.
   ($158.99 vs $125.41 male; $141.95 vs $109.49 female.)
3. **Rate type split.** Every company in the sample is **attained age** except
   **AARP / UnitedHealthcare, which is community rated.** No issue-age policies appeared. Do not
   claim Kentucky has all three types in market without re-checking.
4. **Rate effective dates land in seven different months**: 01/01, 02/01, 04/01, 04/15, 05/01,
   06/01, 08/01. This is the hard proof for "a price comparison is a snapshot." A list that is
   right in August can reorder by November because a carrier's own increase lands in between.
5. **Years in market runs 1 to 60.** Chubb/INA 1, Aflac 4, Healthspring 6, Humana Achieve 7,
   Bankers Fidelity 12, Medico 20, United World (Mutual of Omaha) 22, AARP/UHC 28, Guarantee
   Trust 42, Anthem KY 60. The cheapest is not the newest and the newest is not the cheapest,
   which is the honest version of "look at time in market."

**The employer-carrier point, in numbers.** Anthem's Plan G was second cheapest at $168.66;
Humana Achieve was $214.81. Same standardized policy, about $46 a month apart, roughly $550 a
year. Someone who carried Humana at work and assumes they should stay with Humana is choosing on
a logo. Frame as arithmetic on identical benefits, never as a knock on a carrier, and note it can
reorder next year.

## 3. Caveats that must appear on the page

- **Medicare Select.** Two of the cheapest entries (Anthem $168.66, AARP $192.89) carry a
  Medicare Select flag, meaning a hospital network applies. Quoting the low end without saying so
  would be misleading.
- **"Level 2" AARP rows** ($605, $660 on G; $568, $577 on N) are underwritten rate classes, not
  what a healthy applicant is offered. Excluded from every published range.
- **Household discounts (6% to 12.3%) and policy fees ($20 to $25)** move the real ranking. A
  headline premium is not the price.
- **Preferred vs Standard class.** Bankers Fidelity showed both ($158.99 vs $190.79). Health
  answers decide which you get, outside your Medigap open enrollment window.

## 4. Compliance, for Austin to resolve

1. **State advertising filing.** Medicare Supplement advertising is regulated by the Kentucky
   Department of Insurance, not CMS, and the NAIC Medicare Supplement model regulation requires
   advertisements to be filed with the commissioner in adopting states. A public page naming
   carriers and price ranges is likely an advertisement. **Confirm with KY DOI or your compliance
   contact whether this page requires filing before or after it goes live.** Not a legal opinion.
2. **Appointments.** The page names companies Austin may not be appointed with. It carries a line
   saying we cannot offer every company and that a shopper should check more than one source.
3. **No CSG output reproduced.** Ranges only, rounded, in our words, dated, per the handling rule
   above.
