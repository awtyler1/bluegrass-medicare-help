Got the Baptist Health Letter? What It Means for Medicare Advantage PPO Members in 2027

Austin Tyler, MBALicensed Medicare Advisor, Tyler Insurance Group · November 17, 2026 · 8 min read

Updated November 17, 2026. Based on Baptist Health's October 2026 letter to patients and its published Medicare Advantage network list. Baptist's list can change before January 1, so confirm your exact plan at BaptistHealth.com/MedicareAdvantage before you decide. The Annual Enrollment Period ends December 7.

If you are on a Medicare Advantage PPO and have been seen at Baptist Health in the past year, you may have received a letter this fall titled Important Information About Your Access to Medical Care from Baptist Health. It is short, and it is easy to set aside. Please don't. It means that starting January 1, 2027, the Baptist doctors and hospitals you may have used for years will stop scheduling you, unless you change plans or qualify for an exception.

The short version:

  • What changed: Baptist Health will not schedule appointments on or after January 1, 2027 for patients in Medicare Advantage plans that are out of its network, and the letter says that explicitly includes PPO plans.
  • Why it hits PPO members: a PPO pays for out-of-network care, so many people kept using Baptist and paid a little more. That only worked because Baptist agreed to see them. It is no longer agreeing.
  • Which plans: at the company level, Baptist lists UnitedHealthcare, Wellcare, Essence and Devoted Medicare Advantage as out of network, and Humana, Aetna and Anthem as in network. If you got the letter, Baptist's records show your plan is out.
  • What still works: emergencies are always treated and covered. Patients in active treatment for a serious or complex condition can ask for an exception.
  • Your best window is now: switch to a plan that includes Baptist by December 7, or decide to keep your plan and use other hospitals. The January to March window is a backup.

What the letter actually says

Here is the key paragraph, word for word:

"Baptist Health is not scheduling appointments for dates of service beginning Jan. 1, 2027, for patients who are enrolled in out-of-network Medicare Advantage plans, which include out-of-network Medicare Advantage PPO plans. Exceptions will be made for patients who are undergoing an active course of treatment for a serious or complex medical condition."

Baptist Health patient letter, October 2026

The letter applies to Baptist Health hospitals, physicians, advanced practice providers, specialists and outpatient facilities. Baptist's stated reason is that out-of-network plans "impose coverage determinations, prior authorization requirements, out-of-network restrictions and benefit limitations" that delay care and make it hard to get services approved. That is Baptist's side of a long-running dispute; the insurers have disputed similar claims in the past. For you, the practical effect is the same either way.

What changes for PPO members

The difference between an HMO and a PPO is that a PPO will pay something toward care from providers outside its network. That is why many people in the Lexington area chose PPOs: they could keep seeing Baptist doctors even when their plan did not have a contract with Baptist, at a higher copay or coinsurance.

But that arrangement always had a catch, and Medicare spells it out plainly: in a PPO, "out-of-network doctors, hospitals, and other providers may decide not to treat you, even if you've used them before." Your plan can be willing to pay; it cannot make a provider without a contract agree to see you. Baptist's letter is Baptist making that choice for 2027.

Through December 31, 2026
  • Out-of-network PPO members could usually still book with Baptist
  • The plan paid its out-of-network share; you paid more than in-network
  • Emergencies covered
Starting January 1, 2027
  • Baptist will not schedule appointments for out-of-network plans, PPOs included
  • Exception: active treatment for a serious or complex condition
  • Emergencies still treated and covered

Emergencies are not affected. Hospitals must treat a medical emergency, and every Medicare Advantage plan must cover emergency and urgently needed care, in network or not, at in-network cost sharing. If you are having a heart attack or a stroke, go to the nearest emergency room, Baptist included.

Two things to read carefully in that table. First, being with an in-network company is not a guarantee that your plan is in network; carriers sell several plans, and networks can differ between them. Second, if you received this letter, Baptist's own records say your current plan does not participate, whatever company it is with. Believe the letter, then verify.

Are you an exception?

The letter says Baptist will make exceptions for patients "undergoing an active course of treatment for a serious or complex medical condition." It does not define those words, so do not assume. If you are in the middle of cancer treatment, recovering from surgery, in a cardiac program or seeing a Baptist specialist for something ongoing:

1Call your Baptist doctor's office now.Ask whether you qualify for the exception, for how long, and whether it covers your hospital and outpatient visits as well as the doctor.
2Call your plan.Ask how it will pay for continuing care at Baptist in 2027, what your out-of-network cost will be, and whether it offers any transition-of-care help for people mid-treatment.
3Get it in writing, from Baptist and from the plan, before December 7, so you still have time to switch plans if the answer is no.

Your options before January 1

1Switch to a plan that includes Baptist, by December 7.During the Annual Enrollment Period you can move to any Medicare Advantage plan in your county. Look at plans from companies Baptist lists as in network, then confirm Baptist is in network for that exact plan in 2027, and check that your other doctors and your prescriptions work too. A new plan takes effect January 1, so there is no gap.
2Move to Original Medicare with a supplement and a drug plan.Baptist, like nearly every doctor and hospital, accepts Original Medicare, with no network to worry about again. The catch in Kentucky: leaving Medicare Advantage for a Medigap plan usually means answering health questions, and you can be charged more or turned down. Kentucky's birthday rule does not help you here. There is one exception: if you joined Medicare Advantage for the first time within the last 12 months (when you first became eligible, or after dropping a supplement), a federal trial right lets you get a supplement without health questions. Read switching from Medicare Advantage to Medigap before you drop anything.
3Keep your plan and use a different hospital system.If your doctors are elsewhere or you are willing to move, your plan may already work well. In Lexington, UnitedHealthcare and Wellcare were in network at UK HealthCare and accepted at CHI Saint Joseph in 2026; confirm their 2027 status by name. Our Lexington hospital network guide has the details and phone numbers.
4Missed December 7? There is a backup.From January 1 to March 31, anyone already in a Medicare Advantage plan gets one switch, to another Advantage plan or to Original Medicare. The change starts the first of the following month, so January appointments could still fall in the gap. A hospital leaving at the start of a plan year does not usually create a special enrollment period, so do not count on one.

Before you switch, check these five things

  • Baptist by name. Use the plan's 2027 provider directory, then call Baptist to confirm. Ask about the specific hospital and doctor you use.
  • Your other doctors. A plan that adds Baptist can drop someone else you see.
  • Your prescriptions. Enter every drug in Medicare's Plan Finder; the formulary and drug costs change with the plan.
  • HMO or PPO. An HMO usually covers nothing out of network except emergencies and urgent care, and may need referrals. Know which you are choosing.
  • The out-of-pocket limit. It is in the plan's documents and is the most you would pay in a bad year. See our guide to the five cost terms.

Common questions

Why did I get a letter from Baptist Health about my Medicare Advantage plan?

Baptist Health sent the letter in October 2026 to patients it treated in the past year whose records show a Medicare Advantage PPO plan that does not participate in Baptist's network. It explains that Baptist will not schedule appointments for dates of service beginning January 1, 2027, for patients in out-of-network Medicare Advantage plans, including PPOs.

Which Medicare Advantage plans does Baptist Health accept for 2027?

At the company level, Baptist Health lists Humana, Aetna and Anthem Medicare Advantage as in network, and UnitedHealthcare, Wellcare, Essence Healthcare and Devoted Health as out of network. Passport by Molina is in network only at Baptist Hardin, Louisville and La Grange. Individual plans can differ, so confirm your exact plan at BaptistHealth.com/MedicareAdvantage or with the plan.

Can I still use Baptist Health with my PPO if I pay the out-of-network cost?

Not for scheduled care starting January 1, 2027, unless you qualify for Baptist's exception for active treatment of a serious or complex condition. A PPO pays toward out-of-network care, but out-of-network providers can decide not to treat you, and Baptist has said it will not schedule out-of-network Medicare Advantage patients, PPOs included.

Will Baptist Health still treat me in an emergency?

Yes. Hospitals must treat medical emergencies, and Medicare Advantage plans must cover emergency and urgently needed care in or out of network at in-network cost sharing. The scheduling change affects appointments, not emergencies.

What should I do if I want to keep seeing Baptist Health doctors?

Before December 7, 2026, switch to a 2027 Medicare Advantage plan that includes Baptist Health, after confirming Baptist, your other doctors and your prescriptions for that exact plan. Or move to Original Medicare with a Medigap plan and a drug plan, which Baptist accepts, keeping in mind that Medigap insurers in Kentucky can usually ask health questions when you leave Medicare Advantage.

What if I miss the December 7 deadline?

From January 1 to March 31, 2027, people in a Medicare Advantage plan can make one switch, to another Medicare Advantage plan or to Original Medicare. The change takes effect the first of the following month, so appointments in the meantime may not be scheduled at Baptist.
